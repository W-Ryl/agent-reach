### 1. What the video says
**Who:** **Ali Alqaraghuli**, who calls himself a "PhD space systems engineer". Public bios say: PhD at 26, NASA JPL postdoc/systems engineer at 28, now CEO of two businesses.

**His pitch, in order:**
- **The problem:** your to-do list "just keeps getting longer and longer," and you're "not actually moving the needle on your business."
- If you're "higher up on the [ADHD] spectrum," you get **paralyzed** when things feel overwhelming, but you also get excited and try to do many things in parallel.
- **The key claim, shown on screen as `Performance ⚡ADHD⚡ Knowledge`:** "What you have is **not a knowledge problem**." You already know what to do; ADHD sits between knowing and doing.
- His own story: he knew he was smart, but felt frustrated and "kind of firefighting," and none of the programs he tried worked. So he built his own system, **"Four Steps to Hyperfocus."**
- **Results he claims:** he "goes to sleep every single night with a clear to-do list" and "feels on top of things."
- **Call to action:** "the system that helped over 20,000 ADHD entrepreneurs." His site now says 30,000+.

### 3. Each step explained

**Step 1: Subtract (remove overwhelm first)**
- His core idea: **"productivity is subtractive."** Before adding any system, remove what drains time and attention.
- His sales page says the first video is about "subtracting things that create overwhelm."
- Examples given: quick changes to your **phone, environment, calendar and self-talk**, such as cutting notifications, distracting apps and clutter, and dropping unnecessary commitments and tasks.
- The point is to make you feel less overwhelmed and get small wins first, which build momentum.

**Step 2: Add the "ADHD System" (four pillars)**

| Pillar | What he teaches (public sources) |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Set a clear, specific goal or target and know *why* it matters. You can't prioritize without one. |
| **Prioritization** | Find the **obstacle** blocking that goal, then pick the **one action that removes it**. That action is your priority. Everything else waits. |
| **Time** | An **"opt-out" approach.** Instead of asking "when should I do this?", ask **"why can't I do this right now?"** If there's no strong reason, do it now. Only schedule it if there's a real reason to wait. The paid version is **calendar-based** and aims to improve "time awareness." |
| **Execution** | Deal with the **psychological** blocks: negative self-talk, fear of failure, perfectionism. Use logic on yourself ("If I fail at this, is it the end of the world? Can I just try again?"). He treats failure as iteration, the way SpaceX does. |

**Step 3: Divide (inferred)**
Not explained in the free material. Given the diagram and his "paralysis" framing, it almost certainly means **breaking big, scary tasks into tiny next actions**, small enough to start immediately. This is the standard ADHD fix for "task initiation" problems. It may also mean dividing the day into time blocks on the calendar.

**Step 4: Multiply (partly inferred)**
His public video says that "by subtracting distractions and adding a tailored system, you can **multiply** your productivity," and that **consistency** is the key. So Multiply is most likely about **compounding**:
- a **daily routine** you stick to ("every single day, stick to it")
- matching your schedule to your **chronotype** (when you naturally have energy)
- **reviewing what isn't working** and adjusting

His public points on structure:
- "just enough structure, without being rigid"
- "know thyself": your behaviors and energy patterns
- a **bespoke** system rather than copying someone else's

### 4. What the paid product actually is (public info only)
- **Name:** "4 Steps to Hyperfocus" / "ADHD Entrepreneur" (site: https://www.nextlevelsystems.co/)
- **Price:** **$19 one-time** (no subscription), with a lifetime money-back guarantee.
- **Format:** **bite-sized videos (about 5 minutes each), about 45 minutes in total.** Each comes with immediate action items for your phone, computer, calendar and self-talk.
- **Core:** a **calendar-based system** for clarity, starting tasks, and time awareness.
- He also runs a Skool-based community or group program.

**Honest take:** the system itself is simple and lines up with well-known ADHD strategies: cut distractions, have one clear priority, break tasks down, use a calendar, and fix self-talk. The "double your productivity" and "NASA engineer" framing is marketing, and no independent evaluation of the results was found. At $19 it's cheap to try, but **Jarvis can automate most of it**, which is the interesting part.

### 5. How Jarvis could run this system for you
This is a strong fit. Each step maps to something an assistant does better than a person with ADHD doing it alone.

| Step | Jarvis feature |
|---|---|
| **Subtract** | **"Focus mode" command:** silences notifications, closes distracting apps and tabs, sets phone Do Not Disturb. A weekly **"what can we drop?"** review of tasks and commitments that suggests deleting or delegating. |
| **Clarity** | **Morning check-in by voice:** "What's the one goal for this week? Why does it matter?" Jarvis stores it in memory and repeats it back whenever you drift. |
| **Prioritization** | **"What's blocking that goal?"** Jarvis asks, then picks **one top action** for today from your task list. It shows only that one, not the whole list. |
| **Time (opt-out)** | When you add a task, Jarvis asks **"Can you do this right now?"** If yes, it starts a timer. If no, it asks why and **puts it on your calendar** at a specific time. It can also give time-awareness nudges ("25 minutes left in this block"). |
| **Execution** | **Self-talk coach:** when you're stuck, Jarvis runs the "what's the worst that happens? Can you retry?" script, then suggests a 5-minute starter. It **notices avoidance** (the same task postponed 3 times) and gently asks about it. |
| **Divide** | **Auto task-splitting:** "Break 'launch website' into steps." Jarvis returns tiny next actions, each under about 15 minutes, and only shows the first one. |
| **Multiply** | **Routines and reviews:** a fixed morning briefing and evening shutdown. "Go to sleep with a clear to-do list" becomes an **evening voice review**: what got done, what moves to tomorrow, and tomorrow's single top task. A **weekly review** finds what isn't working and adjusts. It also learns your energy hours and schedules hard work then. |

**How this connects to earlier parts:**
- **Voice:** Voicebox or Hermes voice (Part 2). Coaching works much better spoken than as text.
- **Morning briefing and proactive check-ins:** the Mark LV ideas (Part 2, #2) and the Jev/Nūs "what's on my plate" demo (Part 1, #1).
- **Memory of your goals and patterns:** a notes vault plus context engineering (Part 2, #3; Part 3, #4).
- **Premortem before big commitments:** a natural part of "Subtract" and "Clarity" (Part 2, #4).
- **Error analysis:** track which nudges actually work for you and drop the rest (Part 3, #5).

**Suggested Jarvis skill files to write** (one per feature):
`focus-mode.md`, `weekly-subtract-review.md`, `morning-clarity-checkin.md`, `pick-one-priority.md`, `opt-out-scheduler.md`, `task-splitter.md`, `stuck-coach.md`, `evening-shutdown.md`.
